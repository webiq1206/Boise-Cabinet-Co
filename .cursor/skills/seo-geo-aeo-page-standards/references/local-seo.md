# Local SEO build rules

Apply these on top of the universal core whenever a page targets a place, a service area, or local "near me" demand. The biggest risk in local work is thin, keyword-swapped city pages, so these rules exist as much to prevent doorway pages as to win local visibility.

## NAP and entity consistency

- Name, address, and phone must match the Google Business Profile and every citation exactly, character for character. Inconsistency splits the entity and suppresses rankings.
- State the service area or location plainly in visible text, not only in schema.
- Keep one canonical version of the business name across the whole site.

## Location and service-area targeting

- Each location or service-area page targets one clear place and one clear service intent.
- Reflect the location in the H1, title, URL, intro, and a direct-answer block, naturally, not stuffed.
- Cross-link service pages to the locations they serve and location pages to the services offered there (service-to-location hub and spoke).

## The doorway-page test (run before creating any city or location page)

Before building a location page, confirm it carries genuinely unique local information. A page that only swaps the city name into a template is a doorway page and damages the domain. Each location page should include real, place-specific substance such as:

- Local projects, case studies, or photos from that area
- Area-specific details (neighborhoods served, local conditions, regulations, pricing realities)
- Local reviews or testimonials tied to that place
- Genuinely local FAQs

If you cannot supply unique local value, do not create a separate page. Serve the area from a stronger combined page instead. Score each page for content uniqueness, intent uniqueness, and local value; anything that reads as near-duplicate (70 percent or more similar to a sibling) gets rewritten, merged, or dropped.

## Local schema

- Use `LocalBusiness` (or the most specific subtype) with full NAP, `geo` coordinates, `areaServed`, opening hours, and `sameAs` links to profiles.
- Add `Review` or `AggregateRating` only where real reviews exist and are shown on the page.
- Keep schema NAP identical to the visible NAP and the GBP.

## Local trust and proof

Show local proof on the page: reviews and testimonials tied to the area, recognizable local projects, real imagery (not stock), credentials, and any local authority signals. This is what both Google's local algorithm and AI answers use to decide the business is genuinely present and trusted in that market.

## Local AEO

Answer the questions local searchers actually ask: cost in this area, service availability, response times, what to expect, areas covered. Phrase them as question headings with concise answers so they surface in local answer boxes and voice results.

## Local pre-publish additions

- [ ] NAP matches GBP and citations exactly, in visible text and schema
- [ ] Page targets one clear place and intent
- [ ] Genuinely unique local substance present (passes the doorway test)
- [ ] Service-to-location cross-links in place
- [ ] `LocalBusiness` schema with geo, areaServed, hours
- [ ] Real local reviews, projects, and imagery
- [ ] Local FAQs phrased as questions with concise answers
